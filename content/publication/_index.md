abstract: Substance abuse, particularly opioid overdose, continues to devastate rural communities across the United States. Despite growing research on the relationship between physical environments and substance use, the limitations in existing environmental datasets hinder comprehensive, AI-driven analyses of the opioid epidemic. Enhancing data quality and integrating diverse sources are critical for leveraging AI in healthcare and public health decision-making. This study aims to improve the reliability and depth of environmental data by integrating Google Earth's Geocoding and Places APIs with opioid-related healthcare data in rural Alabama. We analyzed opioid-related emergency room (ER) visit rates from 2018 to 2021 and collected high-resolution environmental data on various physical infrastructure features, including car washes, fast food restaurants, gas stations, hospitals, liquor stores, lodging, and pharmacies. By computing the interquartile range (IQR) and identifying the nearest public facilities across 24 Alabama counties—including eight Black Belt counties, eight southern non-Black Belt counties, and eight northern non-Black Belt counties—we developed refined metrics that capture rural environmental characteristics more precisely. Our findings demonstrate that AI-driven analysis of geospatial and community-level data can significantly enhance environmental assessments that are otherwise unavailable through conventional data sources. The improved data quality and novel metrics derived from this study offer valuable insights for healthcare AI applications, supporting data-driven strategies to mitigate opioid-related risks and inform targeted public health interventions in rural communities.
