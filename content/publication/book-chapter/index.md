abstract: Virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) have experienced significant advancements over the past few decades. What began as experimental technology in the mid-twentieth century has now evolved into immersive platforms that blend physical and digital worlds. This transformation has been driven by key developments in hardware and computational techniques, which have enhanced the realism, interactivity, and accessibility of VR/AR systems. Figure 1.1 illustrates the evolution of VR and AR hardware [1]. The introduction of Morton Heilig’s Sensorama [2] in the 1960s provided a glimpse into the possibilities of multisensory immersion, integrating 3D visuals with sound and motion. The journey of wearable VR hardware began with Ivan Sutherland’s “Sword of Damocles” in the 1960s–a head-mounted display (HMD) that could render rudimentary 3D graphics [3]. However, this early system was limited by its weight and mechanical tracking, making it impractical for widespread use. In the following decades, the focus shifted toward creating more lightweight, mobile, and responsive systems.
